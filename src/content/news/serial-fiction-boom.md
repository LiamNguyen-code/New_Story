A century ago, readers waited weeks for the next installment of their favorite story in a magazine. Today they wait seconds — but the appeal of the serial format is exactly the same: a story that unfolds a piece at a time keeps people coming back.

Writers have noticed. Many now plan their stories around twenty short chapters, each with its own small arc and a final line that pulls the reader forward.

## A Format Built For Busy Days

Short chapters fit naturally into small pockets of time. A reader can finish one on a coffee break, another on the train home and two more before bed, without ever losing the thread of the story.

## Emotional Stories Lead The Way

Family drama, second chances and stories about quiet people who finally speak up consistently draw the most readers. These stories are easy to start, hard to put down and satisfying to finish.

> "I tell myself I'll read one chapter. Then it's midnight and I've read eight." — a Midnight Tales reader

Whatever the genre, the lesson is the same: respect the reader's time, and give them a reason to turn the page.
