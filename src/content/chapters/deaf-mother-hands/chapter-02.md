"Sir," Chloe said, and her voice came out louder than she meant it to. Twelve heads turned toward her at once.

Gabriel's eyebrows rose a fraction. Behind her, she heard the head waiter suck in a breath through his teeth.

"I'm sorry," she went on, gripping the edge of the dessert tray. "That bottle — it was opened in the kitchen. I think the cork was bad. Let me bring you a fresh one."

It was a terrible lie and she knew it. The decanter had been on the sideboard all evening. But Sal's hand stopped, the dark red wine trembling at the lip of the crystal, and for one long moment nobody moved.

"A bad cork," Gabriel repeated softly.

"Yes, sir."

He studied her face, then looked past her — at his mother. The old woman had gone completely still, but her eyes were wet and fixed on her son with an intensity Chloe would remember for the rest of her life.

"Sal," Gabriel said without looking away from his mother. "Let the girl take it."

"Gabe, this is a '97 Barolo. There's nothing wrong with—"

"Let her take it."

Sal smiled and set the decanter down. But as Chloe reached for it, his fingers brushed her wrist, cold and deliberate, and he leaned close enough that only she could hear.

"You should be very careful," he murmured, "about the things you think you see."

Chloe carried the wine out of the room with both hands so no one would notice they were shaking. In the kitchen, she poured a little into a saucer and set it on the floor by the back door, where the cook's old cat was waiting for scraps.

The cat sniffed it, turned away, and refused to touch it.
