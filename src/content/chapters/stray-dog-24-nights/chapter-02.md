title: "The Man in the Gray Hoodie"
---

On the nineteenth night, a man in a gray hoodie came in five minutes before closing and bought a single pack of gum.

He paid in exact change. He didn't look at the gum. He looked at Maya's name tag, then at the clock, then out the front window toward the only car left in the lot.

"Long night?" he asked.

"They're all long," Maya said, and gave him his receipt.

He came back on the twentieth night, and the twenty-first. Always five minutes before closing. Always gum. Dana joked that he had a crush. Maya laughed along, but she started walking out to her car with her keys threaded between her fingers.

On the twenty-second night, Ghost was waiting by the car as usual — but he wasn't sitting. He was standing, stiff-legged, his torn ear flat against his skull, staring at the hedge that ran along the back of the lot.

For the first time since she'd met him, Ghost growled.

Maya spun around. The hedge was dark and still. Nothing moved.

"Easy," she whispered, as much to herself as to the dog. "There's nothing there."

She left the sandwich, got in, and locked the doors. Ghost didn't eat. He stayed exactly where he was, still staring at the hedge, as her taillights pulled away.

On the twenty-third night, the man in the gray hoodie didn't come in at all. Somehow, that was worse.
