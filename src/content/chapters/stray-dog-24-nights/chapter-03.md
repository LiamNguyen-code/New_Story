title: "The Twenty-Fourth Night"
---

It was raining on the twenty-fourth night. Maya pulled her hood up, locked the pharmacy door, and hurried across the lot with a sandwich in one hand and her keys in the other.

Ghost was waiting by the driver's door. But when she reached for the handle, he did something he had never done before.

He stepped between her and the car and planted himself there, a low rumble rising in his chest.

"Ghost, move. It's pouring."

He didn't move. His eyes weren't on her. They were fixed on the back seat of her car.

Maya's blood went cold. She leaned closer to the rain-streaked window — and in the dark, behind the driver's seat, something shifted. A gray hood. A pale hand.

She didn't scream. She didn't even think. She backed away one slow step at a time, and Ghost moved with her, never taking his eyes off the car, until her back hit the pharmacy door and her shaking fingers found her phone.

The police arrived in four minutes. The man in the gray hoodie had a record in two states and a roll of duct tape in his pocket. He had disabled her rear door lock three nights earlier.

When the last cruiser pulled away, Maya sat down on the wet curb. Ghost hesitated, then came and sat beside her — close enough, for the first time, that their shoulders touched.

She took him home that night. He has a real name now, a soft bed, and a sandwich of his own every evening.

But every night at 2:07, he still walks to the front window and checks the street, just to be sure.

**The End.**
