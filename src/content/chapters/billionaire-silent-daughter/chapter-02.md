That evening, Rosa waited outside Nathan Ashford's office for forty-five minutes before he finally opened the door.

He was taller than she expected, and more tired. His tie was loosened, his sleeves rolled to the elbows, and there was a framed photograph on his desk of a smiling woman holding a baby in a yellow blanket.

"Mrs. Pruitt says you wanted to see me," he said. "If this is about the salary—"

"It's about Lily," Rosa said. "She's not silent, Mr. Ashford. She's talking. You just haven't been listening in the right language."

He stared at her.

Rosa explained, as simply as she could: the finger-spelling, the way Lily repeated the letters, the way she'd frozen when Rosa caught her. "Somebody taught her," Rosa said. "Somebody who knew sign language. Was it her mother?"

Nathan's jaw tightened. "Claire took classes. She thought it would be good for Lily to learn a second language early." He paused. "What was she spelling?"

"A name. Marcus."

The color drained from Nathan Ashford's face.

"Marcus Reed," he said quietly, "was my wife's driver. He was driving the car the night she died. He walked away with a broken wrist. He told the police a truck ran them off the road."

He sank into his chair, staring at the photograph on the desk.

"Lily was in the back seat," he said. "She saw everything. And for four months, I've had doctors telling me to give her time — while she's been trying to tell us exactly what happened."
