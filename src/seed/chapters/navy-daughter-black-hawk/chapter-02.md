I stepped away from the party and answered on the second ring. "Hale."

"Commander." It was Captain Reyes, my commanding officer, and his voice was tighter than I had ever heard it. "Where are you right now?"

I told him. There was a pause, and the sound of a keyboard.

"There's an open field behind your father's property. Is it clear?"

"It's a golf course, sir. It's—" I stopped. "Sir, what's happening?"

"The rescue team in the Gulf lost their mission planner to a medical emergency forty minutes ago. A fishing vessel with eleven crew is sinking in a storm, and you are the only officer within two hundred miles who has planned a rescue in those conditions. Transport is already in the air. Twelve minutes."

I closed my eyes. Behind me, I could hear my father telling the story about my "boat thing" again to a new circle of guests.

"Understood, sir," I said. "I'll be ready."

I walked back to the party, picked up my bag, and went to find my father. He was mid-sentence when I touched his arm.

"I have to leave, Dad. I'm sorry. Happy birthday."

He sighed loudly enough for everyone nearby to hear. "Of course you do. Something more important than your own family. What is it this time — a boat that needs driving?"

A few people chuckled. I opened my mouth to answer.

And that was when every glass on the long white table began to tremble.
