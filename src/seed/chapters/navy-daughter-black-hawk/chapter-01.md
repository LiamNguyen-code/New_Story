title: "Sixty Candles"

My father's sixtieth birthday party had a string quartet, an ice sculpture shaped like a golf ball, and forty guests who all seemed to know exactly how much everyone else's house was worth.

I arrived in a plain blue dress and flats, carrying a gift I had picked out in an airport. My father met me at the gate with a handshake. Not a hug. A handshake.

"Elena," he said. "You made it. Your mother would be pleased."

My mother had been gone for six years. He still said things like that.

I spent the first hour the way I always did at his parties: smiling at men who asked me if I was "still doing the boat thing," and answering my phone every time it buzzed, which earned me a look from my father across the lawn each time.

My brother, Jason, was the star of the evening. He sold commercial real estate and had just closed a deal my father mentioned in every second sentence. Jason squeezed my shoulder and whispered, "Don't let him get to you," which only made me feel worse.

Then the quartet stopped, someone tapped a glass, and my father stood up to give a toast.

He thanked his friends. He thanked his business partners. He thanked Jason, at length.

Then he raised his glass in my direction and smiled.

"And of course my daughter Elena, who could have been a lawyer, and instead decided to drive boats for the government." The lawn erupted in laughter. "Twelve years, and she's still not an admiral. But we love her anyway."

Forty people laughed. I smiled, because that's what I had trained myself to do.

Then my phone buzzed again — and this time, the caller ID made my stomach drop.
