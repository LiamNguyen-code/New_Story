title: "The Warning After Dessert"
---

Chloe Bennett had been told three things before she stepped into the Costello house: don't speak unless spoken to, don't look anyone in the eye for too long, and never, under any circumstances, touch the wine without being asked.

The dining room was longer than her entire apartment. Twelve men in dark suits sat along a table of polished walnut, and at its head sat Gabriel Costello — younger than she had imagined, with tired gray eyes and a stillness that made the whole room lean toward him.

To his left sat an elderly woman in a navy dress. Her silver hair was pinned neatly, and her hands rested on the tablecloth like two folded birds. No one spoke to her. No one even looked at her.

"My mother," Gabriel said when he caught Chloe watching. "She can't hear you. Just set the plate down."

Chloe nodded and placed the dessert in front of the old woman. That was when she saw it. The woman's right hand lifted, barely an inch off the table, and her fingers moved quickly — a shape, then another, then another.

*Danger. Wine. Him.*

Chloe froze. She had grown up translating for her deaf grandmother in a crowded Brooklyn apartment. She knew those signs as well as she knew her own name.

The old woman's eyes flicked toward the man on Gabriel's right — Uncle Sal, the one who had greeted Chloe at the door with a too-wide smile. Then the hands moved again, more desperately this time. *Please. Tell my son.*

Chloe straightened slowly, her heart hammering. Across the table, Sal lifted the decanter and began to pour Gabriel's glass.

She had two seconds to decide whether a waitress hired for one night was about to throw away her life — or save his.
