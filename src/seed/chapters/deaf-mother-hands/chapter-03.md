It was nearly midnight when the kitchen door swung open and Gabriel Costello walked in alone.

The cook dropped a pan. The dishwasher vanished into the pantry. Chloe stood by the sink with her apron still on, because there was nowhere left to run.

"My mother doesn't trust anyone," Gabriel said. "Thirty years, she doesn't trust anyone. Tonight she held your hand at the door when you left the dining room. So I'm going to ask you once. What did she tell you?"

Chloe swallowed. "She said the wine was dangerous. She said *him*. She was looking at your uncle."

Gabriel didn't react, not outwardly. But something behind his eyes shifted, like a lock clicking open.

"You know how to sign," he said.

"My grandmother was deaf."

He was quiet for a long moment. Then he said something that surprised her more than anything else that night. "I never learned. My father said it made us look weak. I was nine. By the time I was old enough to know better, I told myself it was too late."

He looked down at his own hands as if he had never noticed them before.

"She has been trying to tell me something for years, hasn't she?"

"I think," Chloe said carefully, "she has been trying to tell you a lot of things."

Gabriel nodded slowly. Then he pulled out a chair at the staff table — the cracked plastic one — and sat down across from her like it was the most natural thing in the world.

"Then teach me," he said. "Starting with the word for *thank you*."

Chloe showed him: fingertips to the chin, then out toward the person you're thanking. He copied it clumsily, then turned toward the doorway — where his mother stood in her navy dress, watching, tears running freely down her face.

She signed it back to him. It was the first conversation they had shared in thirty years.

**The End.**
